A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Brockville property

Record which breaker or device will not reset, what was running when it tripped and whether you noticed heat, a smell, buzzing or flicker. Avoid repeated reset attempts, and keep the panel cover shut.

Ask the contractor what the diagnostic visit will cover, what the written note will say about the cause and whether any repair needs a separate approval. Mention any earlier alterations you know of.

What should the assessment establish?

At a home in Brockville, a protective device will not reset. The contractor should name the reason before any part is replaced and describe the checks that led there, telling a circuit fault apart from a worn device or a faulty appliance plugged into it. Earlier alterations, where visible, are recorded, with a note on any bearing they have on the problem. The scope itemizes the repair, the parts and the ESA notification where it applies. Whatever is still unknown is listed too, since the cause decides what work follows.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Should I keep switching a tripped breaker back on to see if it holds?

No. A device that trips again straight away is signalling something, and repeated resetting can make matters worse. Leave it off, note what you saw and ask the contractor how to proceed.

Who decides whether the repair must be finished before my closing date?

The owner decides with the other parties involved, such as the buyer, lender or insurer. The contractor can describe what the assessment found and what is left, but cannot promise a date.
