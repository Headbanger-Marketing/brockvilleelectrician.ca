An extra receptacle does not establish extra capacity

Receptacle locations and circuit design answer different questions. More convenient plug-in points may share the same existing circuit. New appliances or specialty equipment may need a different connection, a dedicated circuit or other assessed work. Give the Licensed Electrical Contractor the equipment information and intended location so the proposal follows the actual use.

Prepare the scope for your Brockville property

Note which receptacles in each room are loose, cracked, discoloured or accept only two prongs, and photograph them from the front without removing any cover. Write down what is normally plugged into each one.

Ask the contractor whether a two-prong location can be changed without opening the wall, what the replacement involves and whether any receptacle should be taken out of use until it is assessed. Ask how the work will be described in writing.

What should the assessment establish?

Each worn or two-prong receptacle on a Brockville job has an entry on what sits behind it, since the device is only the visible part. Those entries record whether a grounding path exists, what protection suits the spot and whether the circuit shows signs of earlier changes. Where records are missing, a note separates what could be confirmed without opening walls from what could not. The work list gives each location, the work there and the ESA notification where it applies. Locations that can wait until after the fixed date are named for the owner as a separate group.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can a two-prong outlet simply be replaced with a three-prong one?

Not necessarily. A three-prong face on a receptacle does not by itself create a proper grounding path, so the contractor should assess the circuit and explain the options before anything is fitted.

What should I do about a loose outlet I still need to use?

Use another outlet where you can until it is assessed. Stop using it at once if you notice heat, discolouration, a smell, buzzing or sparking, and do not tighten or open it yourself. Tell the contractor what you observed.
