A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Brockville property

List the equipment the project truly needs, from existing major appliances to anything delivered on the fixed date, and photograph the labels on the closed panel door without opening it. Note any alterations the seller or previous owner mentioned.

Ask the contractor to state what demand the proposal is sized for, which of your listed items it includes and what the larger option adds that the smaller one does not. Ask whether the extra is needed now or only later.

What should the assessment establish?

Present demand is compared with the planned loads, and the existing supply either already covers them or falls short; the report states which. It also shows how the recommended panel or service was chosen, instead of only naming the largest option. Listed in the scope are the equipment, the work on the incoming supply and any utility involvement, with the ESA notification as a step of its own. Where earlier owners left visible wiring changes in a Brockville house, the record notes what was seen and what remains unknown. Compare any larger option against that record before you agree to it.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is the largest panel option the safest choice when the closing date is near?

Not automatically. A larger option can add cost and work that the project does not need, while a smaller one may fall short of planned loads. Ask the contractor to explain the difference using your own equipment list.

Can I replace only the panel and leave the incoming service as is?

The answer depends on what the assessment finds. A panel can be replaced without changing the supply, but the contractor should say why that is sufficient for your listed loads and whether the utility needs to be told.
