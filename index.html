Before work begins

Work backwards from the date you cannot move.

Is your home older and altered over time? Its walls may hide wiring of mixed ages and methods, with no record of who changed what or when, which can take time to untangle. Even in a newer home, later work may have gone unrecorded.

Whatever the home, list the fixed date, such as a move-in, a closing or an appliance or vehicle delivery, and the electrical items that depend on it. Ask the contractor to say what must be settled first.

Which papers and photos help when nobody kept electrical records?

Collect any earlier ESA paperwork, invoices, inspection reports, seller disclosures and insurer or lender letters, plus photographs of the panel door and its labels. Where no records exist, say so plainly, because the contractor then has more to establish.

Should the work be split so the essential part is finished first?

Sometimes, but the contractor should explain which items the fixed date truly depends on and which can follow. Ask for the written scope to show each stage separately, so that a later stage does not undo an earlier one.

How do I compare quotes when a deadline is close?

Compare the written scopes line by line before the totals. A quote that leaves out the ESA notification, access work or finishing is not the same job, so ask each contractor what is and is not included.

Who must sign off on wiring changes ahead of a closing or move-in?

The owner authorizes the work, and a lender, insurer, condominium corporation or landlord may have its own conditions. A building permit is separate from the ESA notification, so ask the contractor which of them your project involves.

Does sending this form hold a date for my move-in or closing?

No. Any provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor you deal with separately confirms who it is and that it holds a licence, and settles the dates with you directly, along with scope and cost.
